BEAUTY E-COMMERCE · PRODUCT PAGE

Nova Store (نوا استور)

A self-directed practice project: one product page for a cosmetics shop, built around the questions a beauty buyer asks that a clothing buyer never has to — will this shade look like the swatch, what is actually in it, and will it suit my skin?

Role
Self-directed practice project — sole designer
Team
Solo
Scope
One desktop product detail page (PDP)
Industry
Beauty & Personal Care / E-Commerce
Platform
Desktop (Web) · RTL Persian
Tools
Figma
Constraint —This is a practice exercise, not client work — there is no brief, no real inventory, and no users behind it. I kept the scope to a single page on purpose: one page is small enough to actually finish and specific enough that the decisions have to be defensible.
01 · Context & Problem

A cosmetics product page carries more of the purchase decision than most retail pages do. A lipstick cannot be tried on, the colour on a screen is not reliably the colour on a lip, and a single line in the ingredient list — a paraben, a known allergen — can end the decision outright.

On the Persian beauty pages I looked at, all of that information was present but flattened: shade, ingredients, longevity and skin suitability mixed into one long description or pushed into a spec block far below the fold. The buyer has to read the whole page to find the one fact that decides it. The brief I set myself was to give each of those questions a named, reachable place on the page.

02 · Research & Discovery
No user research — a pattern review of Persian beauty storefronts and the cosmetics sections of general marketplaces
The most useful thing I found was not in the page designs but in their customer Q&A sections, where the same question kept reappearing in different words: is the colour on the site the colour I will get? The pages were rarely built to answer it. That pushed me toward treating this as a structural problem — where information lives and how reachable it is — rather than a visual one.
03 · Process
01
Shade selection as a real control, named in text
Five swatches sit directly under the title, and the selected one is also written out (آلبالویی / cherry). Colour alone cannot carry the choice — someone who cannot separate two dark reds still needs to read which one they picked.
02
A buy box that answers "should I", not just "buy"
Original price, discount percentage, final price and remaining stock are grouped in one panel, with the three guarantees — 7-day returns, authenticity, free shipping above a threshold — stacked immediately beneath it instead of in the footer. Price and risk get read in the same glance.
03
Breaking one long page into five addressable sections
Introduction, specifications, how to use, reviews and Q&A each got an anchor in a tab bar under the gallery. A buyer who only wants the ingredient list jumps straight to it rather than scrolling past everything else.
04
Pulling suitability facts out of the prose and into a table
Texture, longevity, coverage, what it is free from, and who it suits became labelled rows in a two-column table. An allergy check should be a glance, not a careful read of a marketing paragraph.
05
Letting the Q&A do work the description cannot
The Q&A block sits as a peer of the specs rather than an afterthought, and it includes the shade-accuracy question answered plainly — the final colour may differ slightly depending on natural lip tone. An honest answer there is worth more than another line of product copy.
06
Reviews weighted by usefulness, not just recency
Each review carries a rating, a date and helpful / not-helpful counts, with pagination instead of an endless list — so a long, specific review can visibly outrank a two-word one.

05 · Impact & Results

No metrics. This was never built or shipped and no one has used it, so there is nothing to measure. What I can point to is what the page structurally does:

Each purchase-blocking question — shade, ingredients, suitability, longevity, returns — has a named location on the page instead of living inside a paragraph.
The buy box keeps price, discount, stock and the three guarantees together as one unit, so cost and risk are read at the same moment.
The tab bar makes all five sections reachable without scrolling through the ones the buyer does not need.
Shade choice is communicated twice — swatch and written name — so it does not depend on colour perception alone.
06 · Reflection
Limiting myself to one page was the right call. A whole store would have let me stay vague; a single page forced every element to justify why it was there.
The thing I would change first: I designed five flat swatch circles and never checked whether they read as "shades of this product" to someone seeing the page cold. A small swatch shown on a lip would probably beat a plain circle.
I wrote the spec table from what I thought a lipstick page should contain, not from what buyers actually ask. Even five conversations would likely have reordered the rows, and possibly cut some.
There is no mobile layout. On a page this dense — buy box, tab bar, paginated reviews — the reflow is the hard part, so skipping it means the design is only half-proven.
