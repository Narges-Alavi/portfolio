COURSE UX · VIDEO LICENSING

Rahafto Academy (Toward the Sun)

Original design of the course page, checkout, and student dashboard for a platform whose lessons are locked behind device-based video licensing — where the real problem started after checkout, not before.

Role
UI/UX Designer — sole designer on the project
Team
Design + Engineering (2 implementation sprints)
Timeframe
June 2025 – December 2025
Industry
EdTech / E-commerce
Platform
Web (Desktop) + Mobile
Tools
Figma
Constraint —Competitors weren't badly designed. Color, type, card layout — most were solid. Where they broke down was one specific moment: none of them clearly explained what happens to a buyer's video access right after they pay. That gap is where we focused.
01 · Context & Problem

Rahafto sells video courses — programming, AI, crypto — in Persian. Unlike most course platforms that unlock content the moment payment clears, Rahafto's videos are locked behind SpotPlayer, a DRM player used widely by Iranian education platforms. After paying, a buyer has to submit a license request for their specific device (Windows, Android, macOS, or Web), wait for approval, and then receive an activation code. If they later reinstall their OS or switch devices, the license breaks and they have to request a new one.

That mechanic has nothing in common with a normal "buy and watch" experience, and no interface had been designed for it before this project. My scope covered three areas: the purchase-decision page (course detail), the checkout and license-request flow, and the student dashboard — courses, licenses, finances, and support tickets. Because this was the first design of the system rather than a redesign, the core challenge was building a mental model from zero for something the average buyer had never encountered before.

02 · Research & Discovery
Audited several Persian and international course platforms · Reviewed recurring patterns in Rahafto's own support conversations
Two concerns came up constantly: before purchase, "Is this instructor actually credible?" — and after purchase, "Why can't I watch the course I just paid for?" Most competitors had solved one of the two, rarely both. A platform's course page might build trust well, or its post-purchase activation might be clear — but almost never both at once. Rahafto's design had to close both ends of that gap.
03 · Process
01
A real lesson, not a promotional trailer
Instead of a short promo clip, we unlocked one full, real lesson from the course. Buyers can judge both the teaching quality and whether they connect with the instructor's style before paying — the exact hesitation that was causing drop-off.
02
Instructor block, directly under the player
Freelance history, year they started teaching, number of enrolled students — we put this directly beneath the preview video instead of in a separate tab nobody opened.
03
Turning license status into a clear color system
Approved (green), Under Review (yellow), and Expired (red) now sit directly next to each course in the dashboard, so a buyer doesn't have to open a support ticket just to check where their license stands.
04
Asking for the device OS at step one of the license form
Buyers previously had no idea why a license request got rejected. Adding a device OS field (Windows / Android / macOS / Web) as the very first step of the form prevented licenses being issued for the wrong device in the first place.
05
Splitting finances into three independent tabs
Installments, refunds, and purchase receipts used to live in one long, mixed list. Paying an installment could bury a receipt or a pending refund. Three separate tabs let each be tracked on its own.
06
A card-based mobile dashboard instead of a compressed table
The desktop dashboard's table layout didn't compress well onto mobile. Each section — courses, licenses, financial status — became a vertical, scrollable card so it stayed readable on a small screen.
04 · Solution

The result opens with a real, unlocked lesson, a license form that asks for the device OS at step one, and a dashboard that shows the status of every license and every installment without requiring a support ticket to find out. Click any screenshot to view it larger.

05 · Impact & Results
Per the support team, once the color-coded license status shipped in the dashboard, the recurring "where's my license?" question dropped noticeably.
According to the content team, placing the instructor block directly under the open preview came through as stronger purchase confidence in review.
Per the finance team's feedback, splitting the financial tabs reduced mix-ups between receipts and pending refund requests.
The device-OS field on the license form was observed by support to reduce mismatched, rejected license requests.
06 · Reflection
Designing for an unfamiliar concept like video licensing showed me that the hard part is sometimes explaining a technical mechanism in plain terms, not the visual layout itself.
Since this was a first design and not a redesign, there was no earlier version to compare against — every decision had to be justified on its own logic and testing, not by a before/after.
The competitive audit made it clear that strong visual design isn't enough on its own — if the post-purchase path stays unclear, good visuals still lose the buyer's trust.
If I revisited this project, I'd add a short walkthrough — a tooltip or a few-second clip — the first time a buyer encounters the license concept, instead of leaving it to trial and error.
