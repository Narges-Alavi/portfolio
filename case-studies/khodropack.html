Khodropack

A directory app for finding an auto-repair shop in Iran — the first product I ran alone from research to spec.

Constraint —No design team to critique with, no existing system to inherit, and a market where nobody expected to find pricing information online before walking in.
01 · Context & Problem

Users search for a shop by service (timing belt, A/C, electrical…), compare on price and rating, and book an available slot. Khodropack is two-sided: car owners discover and compare shops, and shop owners self-onboard — listing their brands, services, prices, and working hours without needing a salesperson to set them up. It was my first project shipped end-to-end without a team around me — research through final specs.

02 · Research & Discovery
Called ~10 friends and family and asked how they last found a mechanic
Nine of ten said “I asked someone.” One said “I googled and gave up.” Nobody looked at pricing or ratings — they didn’t expect either to exist online for this kind of service. The product had to earn the tap, not just the search.
03 · Process
01
Put the comparison on the card, not the detail page
First version had price, rating, and specialty on the shop detail page. In testing, people wouldn’t tap through to compare — they’d just leave. Moved all three onto the discovery card itself. If it wasn’t on the card, it didn’t exist.
02
Availability, three versions
V1 was a full weekly calendar per shop — people glazed over. V2 showed only “next available” — too little. V3, the one that shipped, leads with “next available” and expands into a day view only when tapped. This screen went through more iterations than anything else in the product.
03
Dropping the map
The obvious feature for a location app was a map view. I built it, then cut it — people were choosing by trust first and proximity second, and the map buried the trust signals. Map became a filter, not a view.
04
Onboarding a non-technical shop owner
The vendor side had to work for a mechanic, not a marketer. Three short steps — info, services and self-set pricing, then hours — and a small copy-to-all-days shortcut on the schedule so an owner with the same hours every day isn’t asked to fill in the same fields seven times.
04 · Solution

On the car-owner side, a discovery feed where the shop card carries everything a user needs to compare — specialty, price range, rating, response time — without ever tapping in, plus a filter panel and a detail page for the trust-building moment before a call. On the shop-owner side, a three-step onboarding — basic info, brands/services/pricing, and working hours — built so a mechanic with no technical background could list a shop alone.

05 · Impact & Results
First independent project shipped end-to-end, from research through developer-ready specs — both the car-owner app and the shop-owner onboarding flow
Availability screen: 5+ design iterations
Replaced a word-of-mouth process with a comparison-based one, at least for users who found the product
No usage or conversion data available yet — this reflects design intent and process, not measured outcomes
06 · Reflection
Cards did more work in this product than any full screen did. Everything got moved onto cards eventually.
I’d start with the hardest screen next time. I saved availability for the end and ended up redesigning half the product backwards from it.
Working solo without a critique partner slowed me down. I now book design reviews with peers even on projects where I’m the only designer.
