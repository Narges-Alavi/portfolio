01 · Context & Problem

Behfalab's core platform reconstructs how a company's processes actually run from raw event-log data, checks that behavior against the process the company designed on paper — conformance checking — and predicts where a running case is likely headed next. My scope covers three connected surfaces: a dedicated analytics dashboard built for one of Behfalab's national B2B clients (KPI cards, comparative bar charts, and province-level rankings over that client's event-log data), the conformance-checking and prediction views inside the core platform, and, outside the software entirely, creative direction for Iran Process Report — Behfalab's process-management publication and event.

02 · Research & Discovery
Discovery here doubled as data modeling — before a single screen, I mapped what the client's raw event log actually contained: case IDs, activity timestamps, province, and the conformance rules the process was supposed to follow.
The dashboard's structure came from the data model, not from a persona. Once I understood which fields the log exposed, the KPI cards, the comparative charts, and the province ranking table followed almost directly from that — the design problem was choosing which relationships in the data deserved a chart versus a number.
03 · Process
01
One card grammar across dashboard and platform
I designed the client dashboard and the core platform's conformance-checking and prediction views to share the same card, chart, and grid patterns, so a client-specific deliverable could still fold back into Behfalab's core product later without a redesign.
02
Turning sponsorship into something a company can actually compare
Sponsorship is usually explained in a PDF sent on request. I designed it as three pages of a public campaign site instead — a landing page that carries the report’s credibility through past editions and named speaker testimonials, a sponsorship page where all five tiers sit side by side with their price and remaining slots, and a survey page that leads with last year’s findings so an organisation sees the value before being asked to contribute data. The survey page is live; the rest of the set followed the same RTL layout rules.
03
One visual system across screen and print
The stippled Iran map from the report cover became the through-line: it carries across the poster, the sponsor brochure, the five-year retrospective sheet, the launch announcement and the site itself. Since the collateral is what reaches people who never open the software, keeping the deep-crimson palette and the map motif identical everywhere mattered more than varying the layouts.
04 · Solution

The client-facing dashboard and the core platform's conformance and prediction views share one visual language, while Iran Process Report stands apart as a dedicated brand system — the report booklet itself, a three-page responsive campaign site for the 1404 edition, and the print and event collateral around it, all built to carry Behfalab's process-mining narrative to people who never open the product. 05 · Impact & Results
One shared card, chart, and grid language now spans a client-specific dashboard and the core platform's conformance and prediction views, instead of two disconnected visual systems
06 · Reflection
A province-level ranking only means something once you understand how the underlying conformance metric is actually computed — I had to learn enough process-mining vocabulary to argue about chart choice, not just copy a chart type from another dashboard.
Keeping the client dashboard and the core platform's own views visually consistent meant resisting the pull to give the paying enterprise client something more custom — the harder, more useful discipline was building one system both could live in.
Designing a report meant to stand entirely on its own, outside the software, was a different exercise from product UI — page rhythm and narrative order mattered more than component reuse.
