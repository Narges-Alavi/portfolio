FINTECH · PAYMENT UX

Payment

A standalone payment screen exploring how a checkout form can feel trustworthy instead of clinical — a live-updating card preview paired with a bold, glass-and-starfield visual identity.

Team
Solo — self-directed practice piece
Timeframe
Short-form exploration · 2024
Industry
Fintech / E-commerce
Tools
Figma, HTML/CSS
Platform
Desktop (Web)
Constraint —This is a self-initiated concept, not a live product — there's no real payment gateway or backend behind it. The brief I set myself was narrower: can a payment form give live, reassuring feedback without ever feeling less secure?
01 · Context & Problem

Most checkout forms are a static stack of inputs — a person types a card number and gets nothing back until the final "Pay" tap. Transaction details (who's being paid, how much, from where) also usually live outside the form entirely, so the person has to trust the page rather than verify what's in front of them. I designed a single checkout screen that tries to close both gaps at once.

02 · Research & Discovery
Reviewed checkout patterns across common payment gateways and card-entry forms
Almost none of them reflect what's being typed back to the user in a recognizable form — the card stays an abstract set of fields until submission. And the "what am I actually paying for" details (store, amount, acceptor code) are usually tucked into a separate summary the user has to scroll to find, right when they're least willing to keep reading.
03 · Process
01
The card had to become the confirmation, not decoration
Every field mirrors onto a live card face the moment it's typed — number, name, expiry, CVV — in the same layout it'll actually be charged with. The card stops being an illustration and starts being the user's own proof they typed it right.
02
Keeping the transaction in view, not buried in the form
Store name, acceptor code, site address, and the final amount sit in their own block beside the card, so the person can sanity-check what they're paying for without hunting through form fields.
03
A security code that behaves like one
The security-code block includes a refresh control, echoing how one-time codes behave elsewhere — a small signal that this step is protected, not just another static input.
04 · Solution

A single-screen checkout: card details on the left, a live card preview and transaction summary on the right, and one clear "Payment" action at the bottom.

The flagship interaction is the live card preview — the card fills in as the user types, in real time — paired with a transaction panel that keeps the store, acceptor code, and total price visible the entire time, so nothing about the payment is left for the person to take on faith.

Checkout — card form, live card preview & transaction summary
05 · Impact & Results

No usage metrics — this is a concept piece rather than a shipped product. What I can speak to are the interactions it was built to prove out, all working end-to-end:

The card preview updates instantly as each field — number, name, expiry, CVV — is completed.
The transaction summary (store, acceptor code, site, amount) stays visible without any scrolling.
The security-code refresh control works as expected across repeated triggers.
The whole flow holds together as a single screen, with no intermediate steps before payment.
06 · Reflection
Making the card itself the feedback surface mattered more than any other single decision on this screen — it's the one thing that actually replaces the anxiety of "did I type that right?"
Next round, I'd usability-test whether the refreshable security code reads as reassuring or just confusing to someone seeing it for the first time.
I'd also want to check the glass-and-starfield palette against contrast tools rather than my own eye alone, since a payment screen is exactly the wrong place for a legibility compromise.
