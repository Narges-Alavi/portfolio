01 · Context & Problem

Dota is a self-service contract builder for Iranian freelancers, founders, and small businesses. Most freelancers here work entirely without a contract — deals get agreed over DM, which opens the door to scope creep, non-payment, and "just make one small change." Since AI made building things cheap, clients increasingly assume revisions are free too — turning the revision-round clause from a minor legal detail into arguably the most critical clause in a freelance contract. I led the design end-to-end, solo — dashboard, templates library, contract wizard, financial/payment tracking, and settings — and we shipped a working MVP.

02 · Research & Discovery
Talked to freelancers about how they actually agree work
Almost none of them used a written contract at all — deals happen over DM. That's exactly what opens the door to scope creep, "just one small change," and clients who never pay. And since AI made building things faster and cheaper, clients increasingly assume revisions are free — so the revision-round clause, usually a throwaway line, needed to become the clause the whole product was built around.
03 · Process
01
There was no template to redesign — the habit was no contract at all
The competitor wasn't a clunkier contract tool, it was the DM. So the wizard had to be faster and less intimidating than typing out a deal in a chat thread, or freelancers would just keep doing what they already do.
02
Split-screen with a real preview
Questions on one side, the actual live-compiling contract on the other. Every answer highlights the clause it just wrote (live focus). The preview isn't a summary — it's the real document, which matters when the whole point is trust.
03
Making the revision-round clause the centerpiece, not an afterthought
Since AI made "just tweak it a bit" free to ask for, the number-of-revision-rounds clause moved from a legal footnote to the single highest-leverage line in the contract. I designed it as its own explicit step, alongside termination conditions, rather than burying both in boilerplate.
04
Cutting entire sections with conditional branching
I modeled a cognitive-load score per wizard step and used it to justify cutting whole paths, not just individual questions. If a contract has no IP transfer, the user never sees the IP screens — those steps disappear from the wizard entirely.
05
The desktop form and preview took several failed passes
Getting the two-pane layout — form on one side, compiling contract on the other — to stay legible and error-free at desktop width took repeated trial-and-error, and the contract-creation form itself needed the same amount of rework.
06
Legal text at a readable size, not shrunk to fit
The compiled contract and the plain-language wizard copy stay at a readable body size on both panes. This is the document a freelancer actually has to read, not skim, so I resisted the usual instinct to shrink legal text just to make it fit.
04 · Solution

A wizard flow with one plain-language question per step, a live preview showing the contract compile as the user goes, and conditional branches that skip whole sections when they don’t apply.

The flagship feature was the pairing of live preview + live focus — each answer highlights the exact clause it just generated — alongside a payment tracker that lets freelancers follow every contract's payments, whether a single lump sum or an installment schedule.

05 · Impact & Results

No usage metrics yet — the MVP is fresh. What I can speak to are the core scenarios the product was built to handle, all working end-to-end today:

A freelancer can generate a website-design contract for a client in under 5 minutes.
A freelancer can split a project's payment into 3 installments, and the system tracks payment timing on its own.
The client receives and downloads the contract as a PDF.
A freelancer can see the list of their active contracts.
A freelancer can see which installments are overdue.
06 · Reflection
Showing the real document being built was the highest-value decision on this project. Every cheaper preview alternative I tested left the anxiety intact.
Conditional branching is presented as an efficiency feature in most wizards. Here it was the main lever on completion rate — a shorter path just means more people finish.
I'd usability-test the revision-round step in isolation next round. It's the clause carrying the most weight in the whole contract, and I never quite closed the loop on whether freelancers actually understand what they're committing to when they set a number there.
