01 · Context & Problem

Dota is a Persian contract builder for freelancers. Many freelancers in Iran agree on work in DMs and never write a contract. I designed Dota from zero, on desktop and mobile: dashboard, contracts, templates, installment tracker and settings. I led the design alone, and we shipped the MVP in 14 days.

02 · Research & Discovery
Where the problem starts: agreeing on work over DM
The main problem wasn't the contract itself. It was that the deal lives in a DM, where nothing is limited and nothing is tracked. Two real problems come from that: revisions never end, and payments slip. Since AI made building cheap, clients assume changes are free, so the number of revision rounds became the most important clause.

05 · Process
01
There was no template to redesign
The habit was no contract at all, so the real competitor was the DM. The builder had to feel faster than typing the deal into a chat.
02
Form and live preview side by side
The contract builds up next to the form, so the freelancer sees the real document while answering. Live focus highlights the clause each answer changes.
03
Revision limit and termination conditions as real clauses
The number of revision rounds is its own clause, with a number the freelancer sets (Article 5 in the freelance services template). Termination conditions are part of the contract too.
04
One payment or installments
A switch picks a single payment or installments. Each installment has a title, a percent, a due date and an amount, and a running total shows how much of 100% is assigned. The payment tracker follows these installments afterwards.
05
Mobile: two tabs instead of two panes
There is no room for the form and the preview side by side on a phone, so they become two tabs: Edit form and Preview.
06
The desktop form and preview took several failed passes
Keeping the two panes readable and error-free at desktop width took repeated trial and error. The contract creation form needed the same amount of rework.
06 · Solution

A five-step builder: template, basic info, amounts and installments, miscellaneous clauses, and review. The core feature is the live preview with live focus: the contract builds up next to the form, and the clause each answer changes is highlighted. Once the contract is done, the payment tracker follows every installment, whether it is one payment or several.

08 · Impact & Results

The MVP is shipped. These are the core scenarios it was built for, all working end to end:

A freelancer can generate a website-design contract for a client in under 5 minutes.
A freelancer can split a project's payment into 3 installments, and the system tracks payment timing on its own.
The client receives and downloads the contract as a PDF.
A freelancer can see the list of their active contracts.
A freelancer can see which installments are overdue.
09 · Reflection
The revision-round clause turned out to be the clause that carries the most weight. Next time I'd test that step on its own with freelancers, to check they understand what a number there commits them to.
The two-pane form and live preview took the most rework of the whole build.
With the MVP out, the missing piece is usage data. The metrics section stays at baseline until there is real data.
